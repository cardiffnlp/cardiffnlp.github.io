abstract: "LLMs have transformed how we generate, understand, and evaluate language, creating new opportunities for human–AI collaboration while raising fundamental questions about reliability, adaptability, and trustworthiness. As these models become increasingly integrated into real-world applications, particularly education, understanding their capabilities and limitations is essential. In this talk, I will explore recent advances and challenges in language learning, generation, and evaluation. I will first discuss how NLP and LLMs can support language learning and assessment through automated feedback, assessment, and content generation. I will then examine the challenges of evaluating LLM-generated outputs, focusing on the reliability of automated evaluation methods and the detection and understanding of hallucinations. Finally, I will present recent work on BabyLM, exploring how language models can learn effectively from limited data and what this reveals about language acquisition and the development of more data-efficient models."

**Invited Speaker:** [Dr. Zheng Yuan](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~zy249/) (University of Sheffield)

**Short Bio:**
Dr. Zheng Yuan is a Senior Lecturer in Natural Language Processing and Deputy Head of the NLP Group at the University of Sheffield. She is also an Affiliated Researcher at the Alan Turing Institute and maintains academic affiliations with the University of Cambridge and King's College London, including a Fellowship at Trinity College, Cambridge. Her research focuses on NLP, LLMs, and human–AI collaboration, particularly in AI for education, language learning and acquisition, automated assessment and feedback, multilingual NLP, and trustworthy and creative AI. Previously, she held academic positions at King's College London and the University of Cambridge and served as VP of Data Science at Chatterbox Labs. She is actively involved in the international NLP and AI in Education communities, serving as Treasurer of ACL SIGEDU, a member of the ACL Professional Conduct Committee, a (Senior) Area Chair for major NLP conferences, and a board member of the International Alliance to Advance Learning in the Digital Era. She holds a PhD and an MPhil from the University of Cambridge and a BSc (Eng) from Queen Mary University of London.
